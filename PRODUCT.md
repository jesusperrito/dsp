# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

The people actually reviewing this are client-side stakeholders and agency decision-makers deciding whether to greenlight the "Times of the Wild" campaign concept for Dubai Safari Park's season opening. But the piece is written and designed **as if** for the real audience it's pitching to serve — future park visitors and social followers — because staying fully in character is the persuasion device. Two audiences, one artifact: the client reads it, but it has to work as if a visitor found it.

## Product Purpose

A fully-realized concept pitch: a satirical "wildlife newspaper" microsite for Dubai Safari Park's season opening, complete with masthead, sections, bylines, and in-fiction ad breaks. It exists to sell an editorial/campaign idea by letting the client experience the finished voice and craft directly, rather than describing it in a slide deck. Success is the client trusting the concept enough to greenlight it for real production.

## Positioning

It sells the campaign by showing it fully realized, not by pitching it in slides — the client experiences the exact tone, voice, and craft the real campaign would ship with.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: none within the fiction — the ad tiles and Pawdcast mentions are in-world flavor, not real links. The actual conversion this artifact drives is off-page: the client saying yes to production.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this newspaper reads like the animals themselves are running the newsroom.
- Belief ladder (in order): (1) the animal-journalism voice is genuinely funny and warm, not just a gimmick, (2) the newspaper format scales across a full editorial calendar of sections and ad breaks, (3) the visual system (masthead, ticker, stamped-edge cards) feels premium enough for the brand, (4) this is worth greenlighting for real production.
- Proof on hand: none supplied. The artifact itself is the proof — there's no separate case study or testimonial backing it.

## Brand Personality

Playful, satirical, warm. Tabloid-style animal gossip journalism — witty and silly, but affectionate. The jokes land on gentle absurdity (a crocodile's wounded pride, a giraffe's viral haircut), never on mockery.

## Anti-references

None named specifically. Default guard: avoid reading as a generic corporate brochure or a templated "AI pitch deck" — the whole point is that it feels like a real, lived-in publication.

## Design Principles

Show, don't tell: the pitch persuades by being the finished artifact, not by describing one.

In-character consistency: every section — ticker, ad breaks, bylines, credits — stays inside the fiction. Nothing should read as a template placeholder (the Classifieds and comic-strip sections are the current exceptions and are known-unfinished).

Affectionate satire, never mean-spirited: jokes land on gentle absurdity, not on making any animal (or human) the butt of a cruel joke.

Editorial craft signals production-readiness: layout, type, and level of finish need to look real enough that a client can picture this live on day one.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast throughout. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (the ticker already does this — extend the same treatment to any new motion). Nav rail and section folds must stay keyboard-navigable.
